Figure 1. Cohort construction, 24-h landmark, and prespecified database roles. a, MIMIC-IV development and eICU evaluation streams. The diagnosis-priority phenotype comprised 434 MIMIC-IV stays before the landmark and 347 patients at 24 h (31 in-hospital deaths), and 470 eICU stays before the landmark and 359 patients at 24 h (21 deaths). The eICU broad and sensitive phenotypes comprised 1,264 eligible and 1,007 landmark patients (87 deaths), and 1,385 eligible and 1,106 landmark patients (96 deaths), respectively. The strict time-window phenotype was NE because diagnosis event time was unavailable. b, NWICU contributed measurement and score-computability analyses, SICdb an exploratory analysis with 17 deaths, and HiRID a general-ICU measurement-process reference. Counts are aggregate first eligible ICU stays or patients.

Figure 2. Descriptive cross-database measurement-process comparisons. a, Percentage of patients with at least one eligible measure among frozen variables in the aligned primary-AP landmark cohorts (MIMIC-IV, n=347; eICU, n=359). b, Median measures per hour among observed patients; the colour scale uses log(1+rate) for display only. c, eICU-minus-MIMIC-IV difference in presence, in percentage points. NE denotes not estimable or not structurally comparable.

Figure 3. Model performance across the primary and sensitivity eICU cohorts and secondary MIMIC-IV internal diagnostics. a-d, AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, and log loss for Models A and B in the primary eICU cohort after harmonising source sex labels to the model coding (n=359; 21 deaths), the sensitive eICU cohort (n=1,106; 96 deaths), and patient-keyed MIMIC-IV out-of-fold predictions (n=347; 31 deaths). Points show estimates and whiskers show patient-bootstrap 95% confidence intervals; section breaks distinguish the primary external, sensitivity and secondary internal analyses.

Figure 4. External calibration in the primary and sensitivity eICU cohorts, with primary-cohort calibration curves and prediction distributions. a,b, Jointly estimated logistic-recalibration intercept and slope for Models A and B; points show estimates and 95% confidence intervals. c, Logistic-calibration curves for the primary eICU cohort (n=359; 21 deaths), with the ideal line, patient-bootstrap 95% bands, and grouped observed-risk summaries with binomial intervals. d, Aggregate predicted-risk summaries by model and outcome; boxes show the interquartile range, centre lines the median, and whiskers the 5th–95th percentiles. Calibration-in-the-large, estimated with slope fixed at 1, is reported in the text and Supplemental Table S3.
